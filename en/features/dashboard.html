Feature 01

Usage dashboard

Claude Code and Codex limits, split into one card per tool. One line per window, one ticked gauge per line. You don't have to read the numbers — the colour of the bar already tells you whether you have room.

The screen // one card per tool

The card title is the tool being measured. The legend in the top right says what the percent column refers to. Read a line left to right as window name · gauge · percent · reset or run-out time.

The toggle above really works. It is the same setting you'll find in the app.

  • Ticked gauge — drawn as 30 segments instead of one smooth bar. A tick is roughly 3%, so "how much is left" is something you can count.
  • Colour — steps down with what's left: green → lime → yellow → orange → light red → red. You see the colour before you read the number.
  • Window name — the unit the limit actually applies to, written plainly (5 hours, 1 week); per-model limits are labelled with the model name (Fable).

Usage / Remaining // flipping the percent column

Usage is the default. The bar fills as you spend, and the more it fills the closer it gets to red. If thinking in terms of what's left suits you better, switch to Remaining in settings (the gear). The percent column and the bar then both count what's left — the bar still fills from the left, and a long bar simply means "you have room".

Flip the toggle on the cards above and the percent and bar length flip together, 68% ↔ 32%. The colour stays keyed to what's left in either mode, so changing the display never changes how risky the line looks.

  • Usage — fills with what you've spent. The more you use, the longer and redder the bar.
  • Remaining — fills with what's left. Plenty left is long and green; nearly empty is short and red.

Forecast // when you run out at this pace

The translucent stretch painted onto the gauge is the forecast. It takes the pace you've been burning at, carries it to the end of the window, and marks where you'd land. This stretch appears only when you look likely to run out before the reset. A window that will comfortably survive isn't dressed up as a scare — it just shows its reset time.

Running out comes first

Keep this pace and the limit is gone before the reset → the forecast stretch appears, and the text on the right switches from "resets in" to "runs out in", in red.

The forecast can be turned off in settings. The countdown next to it stays either way.

  • It's a projection, not a promise. Change pace and the projected point moves with you.
  • The forecast is drawn translucent and real usage opaque, so measured and estimated never blur together.

At-a-glance warning // noticing without looking

DASH is a screen you stand up beside you and catch out of the corner of your eye. So when what's left drops below a threshold, the edge of the screen glows softly in a warning colour five times, then fades. Without reading a word, the colour at the edge of your vision says "time to start rationing".

The threshold is based on what's left. Across every tool and window, the single most urgent window sets the overall warning level.

Press a button to play that level's warning. Just like in the app, it pulses five times and then fades.

  • ≤50% left — yellow. Half gone. Worth knowing, not worth stopping for.
  • ≤30% left — orange. The last third. Time to ease off.
  • ≤5% left — red. Effectively empty.
  • The warning is light bleeding inward from the edges, so it never covers the gauges in the middle, and it disappears completely after five pulses instead of sitting there tiring your eyes.
