Feature 03

Learns your criteria

"What counts as important mail" is different for everyone. Rather than breaking that down into a page of settings, DASH lets you write it as one plain sentence. The sidebar filters by what you wrote, and senders that keep repeating are raised as candidates by the app itself.

Where to find it // four taps

Criteria are kept per account — what's important in a personal inbox is never what's important at work.

  1. 01Unread sidebar →
  2. 02Gear ⚙ →
  3. 03Edit › on the account line →
  4. 04Important-mail criteria →

You can press through steps 01–03 yourself on the Unread sidebar page.

Important-mail criteria // write a line, apply it

Write one sentence in the Important-mail criteria — settings for this account only box. No format to follow, no keyword list to build. Press Apply and the criteria are saved for this account, and the mail still sitting in the sidebar is sorted again against them.

Example 1 — Only show mail from the last 10 days

  • This account only — the criteria apply to the account you're editing, nothing else.
  • Nothing is deleted — mail that doesn't match simply drops out of the sidebar. In Gmail it's still there, still unread.
  • You can undo it — clear the box and apply again, or press Reset settings, and you're back where you started.

Applies — 10 a day

The 'Apply' button can be pressed 10 times a day across all accounts combined. Not 10 per account — 10 for the whole day. The button shows how many you have left (Apply (7 left today)), and once they're gone they refill the next day. Rather than nudging the wording and pressing again and again, it's better to write one sentence properly and apply it once.

Automatic rule learning // so you don't rewrite it every time

Filtering out the same sender by hand over and over is tedious. DASH keeps a count of the verdicts per sender and raises senders that have come often enough and were never once important as candidates. What happens to a candidate is up to you.

For example

news@letter.example.com sends two or three mails a week. Over three weeks, all 11 that arrived were judged unimportant, and you never opened a single one. → That sender becomes a candidate.

What makes a candidate

Not just any sender becomes a candidate. All of the following have to hold.

  • It has come many times — one or two mails aren't enough to judge on.
  • Steadily, over more than a week — a burst that all arrived in one day doesn't count.
  • Never once judged important — a single important mail takes the sender off the list.
  • Never opened by you either — any sign that you read it and it isn't a candidate.

Each candidate comes with the reason it's a candidate, and if it hasn't met every condition yet, what's still missing and by how much.

Safety nets

  • Hidden mail stays visible — you can see exactly what the automatic rules are covering, down to the subject line, so a bad rule is something you can catch.
  • Undo is permanent — undo a rule and that sender is never excluded automatically again. You don't get the same mistake twice.
  • Apply now works in any mode — even in Observe only, the button beside a candidate excludes it immediately.
